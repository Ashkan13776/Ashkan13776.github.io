About

I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Vanderbilt University, where I work in the Machine Intelligence and Neural Technologies (MINT) Lab with Prof. Soheil Kolouri. My research targets the compute and memory cost of LLM inference: attention that scales, KV caches that hold up over long contexts, and model cascades that send each query to the cheapest model that can handle it.

Much of that work starts from geometry. Optimal transport offers a principled answer to which tokens should attend to which, and it led to ESPFormer (ICML 2025), which computes doubly stochastic attention in a single pass without Sinkhorn iterations, and LOTFormer, which brings it to linear time. My latest project, OVAL, rethinks how KV cache pages are summarized for retrieval during long-context decoding, weighting directions by their effect on the attention output rather than on the keys alone.

I care about these ideas holding up outside the lab. At Instacart, I deployed a multi-LLM cascade for catalog attribute extraction that matches the F1 of running o3 on every product at 75–80% lower cost. With clinical and engineering collaborators, I also build real-time neural simulators of soft-tissue deformation and cutting for surgical simulation (AAAI 2026, U.S. patent application). Before Vanderbilt, I studied Biomedical Engineering at Amirkabir University of Technology in Tehran.

Research

Four threads, one theme: accurate models that run fast and cheap.

Retrieving the right KV pages for long-context decoding

Long reasoning traces and 100K-token contexts make the KV cache the bottleneck. OVAL is a training-free page summary that weights key directions by how much they change the attention output, so retrieval picks the pages that actually matter. It keeps the same format and scoring cost as key-only spectral summaries at equal rank and page size.

Paper: OVAL (first author, arXiv 2026)

  • 9 / 9settings where OVAL is best (one tie) among 8 compressed methods, at a 2,048-token KV budget on DeepSeek-R1-Distill 7B to 14B
  • +7.1percentage points over the strongest baseline on AIME24; +3.7 to +3.9 on average across MATH500, AIME24 and GPQA
  • 87.2%of full-cache accuracy at 100K+ tokens on InfiniteBench with Llama-3.1-8B, versus 83.9% for FreeKV

Attention built from optimal transport

Softmax attention normalizes only one way. Doubly stochastic attention balances both queries and keys, but usually needs costly Sinkhorn iterations. ESPFormer gets there in one pass using expected sliced transport plans. LOTFormer makes it linear-time with low-rank transport through learned pivots, and LUNA learns the kernel of linear attention itself, with generalization guarantees.

Papers: ESPFormer (first author, ICML 2025), LOTFormer (first author), LUNA (co-first author)

  • +2.6top-1 points over softmax on ImageNet-1K with DeiT-Tiny at comparable FLOPs (72.2 to 74.8), from LOTFormer
  • 1 passdoubly stochastic attention with no Sinkhorn loop; ESPFormer beats softmax and Sinkformer on image, point-cloud, sentiment and translation tasks
  • O(n)cost in sequence length for LOTFormer and LUNA; converting fine-tuned BERT and ViT-B/16 to LUNA recovers most of their softmax accuracy

Cost-aware LLM cascades in production

As an ML PhD intern on Instacart's Content AI team, I built and deployed a multi-LLM cascade for catalog attribute extraction: cheap models answer first, and only uncertain items escalate. I set the escalation rule by benchmarking three confidence estimators on a golden set, then distilled them into a lightweight five-level verifier that costs a fraction of the ensemble.

Instacart, Content AI, Sep to Nov 2025

  • 75–80%lower cost per item while matching the F1 of running o3 on every product
  • 150Kproducts, with 50+ attributes extracted per catalog
  • 147Klabeled samples in the golden set used to choose the escalation rule

Real-time neural simulators for surgery

Finite element models are accurate but too slow for live surgical guidance. I train physics-informed neural models on FEM data to predict soft-tissue deformation and cutting in real time, from tissue handled by multiple graspers (Neural Augmented Kelvinlet) to whole organs with resection (SurgFormer).

Papers: Neural Augmented Kelvinlet (co-first author, AAAI 2026), SurgFormer (co-first author, arXiv 2026), U.S. patent application 63/998,405

  • TissueNeural Augmented Kelvinlet models multi-grasper soft-tissue deformation in real time (AAAI 2026)
  • OrganSurgFormer scales to whole organs, including topological changes from resection, at real-time inference
  • Patentapplication filed for hybrid AI-driven, physics-informed soft-tissue simulation
